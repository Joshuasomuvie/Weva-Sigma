01 / 25 · The Opening

You’ve built solutions for businesses. Let’s make sure the businesses that need them can find them.

Every business exists to solve a problem. Some help people access products. Others make everyday activities easier. Some provide the systems that allow other businesses to operate better — and Weva is building in that last category.

Across its products, Weva addresses challenges businesses meet as they grow: managing customer relationships, organising internal operations and maintaining financial visibility. These affect how businesses operate, make decisions and grow.

Creating a solution and getting that solution into the hands of the people who need it are two different challenges.

A valuable product can exist without reaching its intended market. The people who would benefit may never discover it. That is the opportunity we want to explore with you.

This presentation covers what we understand about Weva, what our external market investigation found, where we see opportunities and how Sigma proposes to approach customer acquisition.

Our objective

Help more of the right businesses discover, understand, adopt and eventually pay for the solutions Weva is building.

04 / 25 · The Business Opportunity

A useful product does not automatically become a widely adopted product.

There are three separate challenges in software growth.

01BuildSomething that solves a real problem.Product development
02ReachPeople who experience that problem.Market understanding, positioning, acquisition
03AdoptConvince them to adopt and keep using it.Conversion and customer adoption

A product can be technically capable but commercially difficult to sell if its audience does not recognise the problem, understand the solution or see enough reason to change its current approach.

Weva’s next growth opportunity should not be approached as a simple exercise in running advertisements. It should be approached as a customer acquisition strategy.

05 / 25 · Our External Market Findings

The problems Weva addresses already exist in the market. But so do alternatives.

External researchWe examined the business problems behind the three products, the businesses likely to experience them, existing software alternatives and the informal methods businesses use today. Three market realities stood out.

Finding 1Businesses already have ways of managing these problems.

A business may use WhatsApp Business for customer conversations, spreadsheets for information, WhatsApp groups to coordinate employees and notebooks to record transactions. These methods may be fragmented, but they are familiar.

A potential customer does not automatically adopt new software because it exists. The product must show a sufficiently meaningful improvement over the current approach.

Finding 2The problems become more important as businesses grow.

A business with a few enquiries may not need a dedicated sales management system; one with multiple salespeople and high enquiry volume has a stronger reason. Likewise, a small team may manage through verbal instructions, while a growing company may struggle to keep processes consistent.

The size and intensity of the problem matter.

Finding 3Software adoption requires more than awareness.

Potential customers may worry about cost, complexity, usability, data security, migration and whether employees will actually use the software.

So advertising should not only say what a product does. It must explain why it is relevant, how it works and what adoption looks like.

Our conclusion

Weva is not simply competing for advertising attention. It is competing against existing habits, established software alternatives and the perceived inconvenience of changing how a business operates. That is what the acquisition strategy must address.

07 / 25 · The Strategic Gap

Three products. Three different problems. Three different reasons to buy.

External researchLeadrix, Harmoni and Fintrix should not be marketed to one generic audience called “SMEs”. Their customers have different responsibilities, motivations and buying triggers.

A business owner who needs help managing customer enquiries may have no immediate interest in employee onboarding software. The products may belong to one ecosystem, but their acquisition journeys should reflect their individual markets.

Our recommendation: one Weva brand, with a distinct acquisition strategy for each product.

08 / 25 · Audience Intelligence Mapping

Don’t advertise blindly. AIM.

Effective advertising begins before the advertising platform is opened. It begins with understanding the audience.

The eight layers of AIM — select a layer to trace the path

What AIM is

Our proposed process for identifying commercially relevant audiences, understanding their business circumstances, examining their problems and aligning those findings with an appropriate advertising message.

It is not simply demographic targeting or selecting interests inside an ad platform. It is an intelligence process.

Why AIM matters to Weva

Without this understanding, advertising becomes repeatedly showing software ads to broad groups and hoping the right customers respond.

With AIM, campaign decisions begin with a reason: identify the audience, understand the problem, determine the motivation, develop the message. The campaign is built around the buyer’s business reality rather than the software’s feature list.

15 / 25 · Creative Strategy

We want prospective customers to recognise their problems before we introduce the product.

We recommend five creative approaches.

01 · Problem recognition

Introduces a recognisable situation before showing the product.

Example — Leadrix“How many customers have contacted your business this week that nobody has followed up with?”
02 · Consequence-led

Highlights the consequence of an existing business process.

Example — Harmoni“If your business cannot function without you answering every little question, you may have an operations problem.”
03 · Contrarian education

Challenges an assumption and creates interest in the financial problem.

Example — Fintrix“Making more sales doesn’t necessarily mean your business is making more money.”
04 · Product demonstrations

Show the actual product interface and explain how a specific workflow operates, so the viewer understands the product through demonstration rather than promotional descriptions.

Needs Weva input

Uses Weva’s real product — no mock-ups are shown in this document.

05 · Objection handling

Acknowledges existing alternatives and explains the specific circumstances in which the product could be useful.

Example — Leadrix“Why would you need a CRM when you already have WhatsApp Business?”
Initial creative library, per active product
  • 3 problem-recognition videos
  • 2 consequence-led videos
  • 2 product demonstrations
  • 2 objection-handling videos
  • 2 static or carousel concepts

Concepts for structured testing — not advertisements that must all run simultaneously.

21 / 25 · Decision-Making

We will not scale campaigns simply because they generate cheap clicks.

Our optimisation decisions follow the evidence.

What we observeWhat we investigate
High impressions, low engagementAudience and creative relevance
High clicks, low registrationsLanding page and conversion friction
High registrations, low activationOnboarding and product usability
High activation, low paid conversionProduct value, pricing and commercial follow-up
Low acquisition cost, poor retentionAudience quality and product fit
Strong activation and paid conversionWhether increased investment is economically sustainable

Not every conversion problem is an advertising problem. If users register but cannot understand how to use the product, increasing advertising expenditure may only increase the number of inactive accounts. We identify the point of friction and recommend the appropriate improvement.
